### 2.9 The prefetch that fits: K slabs staged through LDS at wave32 — **done** ✅ (2026-09-29)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G2, after §6.5): the shipped fp16 GEMM
(`dit_gemm_wg128x256_bt16_swz8`, four wave64 waves of 4×8 accumulator
tiles, one K tile a slab) pays one exposed load latency a K tile and
runs at 74% of the ceiling at its clock; with every load an L0 hit it
reaches 82–85%. Can the next tile's loads be issued before this tile's
MMAs, bit-identically, inside the register file?

**Answer: not as fragments, as a slab.** The shipped build is now
`dit_gemm_wg128x256_lds_w32`: eight wave32 waves of 4×4 tiles in the
same 128×256 workgroup, the K slab (two 16-wide tiles) fetched by the
workgroup as 6 `uvec4` a lane into 24 staging registers, in flight for
the whole of the previous slab's MMAs, written to the other of two LDS
planes behind them, one barrier a slab, and the fragments read from LDS
just ahead of their MMAs. Same MMAs in the same order per accumulator,
so **bit-identical** (`TestGPUShapes`'s `H3_GEMM_REF` gate against the
wave64 build's velocities; `TestGPUForward`).

**480p H3 forward** (`H3_SHAPES=480 H3_PROFILE=1 H3_BANK=q8`, the
machine otherwise idle, 100 ms clock and package-power samples; per clock
is FLOP/clk/CU against the probe's 480):

| build | qkv | gate / up | o | down (KRANGE) | forward | sclk | per clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped wave64 4×8, one tile | 37.2 | 37.3 / 37.1 | 35.4 | 28.7 | 29.86 s | 2620 | 355, **74%** |
| **`lds_w32`** (+ its KRANGE companion) | **41.1 / 40.9** | **40.7 / 40.7** | **38.9 / 39.1** | **40.8 / 40.6** | **27.16 / 27.11 s** | 2665 | 385, **80%** |

Two runs of the winner agree to 0.2%. The down projection, whose 235 MB
A chunk overflows the MALL and which M11d could only take from 26 to
29, is at the other projections' rate: the workgroup reads its A slab
once instead of once a wave. All at 154 W, the package cap.

**How it was found: the ladder, every rung bit-identical, in order.**

| arm | build | qkv | per clock | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SR`: strength-reduced addresses (§6.5's 85 instructions a tile → 12 scalar adds + 12 lane folds) | wave64 4×8 | 37.2 | 74% | **nothing**: the address arithmetic was never the cost |
| `PIPE`: A0/A1 double-buffered in the 16 spare VGPRs, A2/A3 and every B reloaded in place after their last MMA | wave64 4×8, 252 VGPRs | 34.3 | 68% | **dead on this compiler**: ACO cannot coalesce a loop-carried cooperative-matrix fragment; every one crosses the back-edge as ~16 `v_mov_b16` (200 + 134 moves a two-tile trip) |
| `DB`: a full second fragment set, the next tile issued before this tile's MMAs | wave64 4×4, 8 waves | 36.7 | 73% | the same moves (128 + 66); not a measurement of the idea |
| the AI-32 control: 4×4, one tile, no prefetch | wave64, 8 waves | 32.6 | 64% | the intensity drop from 43 to 32 costs 13% |
| `k2`: 4×4, two-tile hoisted slab (`BK_TILES=2 HOIST_A=1`), no carried fragments | wave64, 8 waves, 192 VGPRs | 38.4 | 77% | +18% over its control at the same intensity: the prefetch works when the compiler is not asked to carry it |
| `k4`: four tiles hoisted | wave64, 256 VGPRs | 37.2 | 72% | no better than two |
| `LDS_STAGE` at wave64 4×8, pitches 24 / 16 halves | 256 VGPRs, 36 / 28 KB | 31.0 / 25.5 | 58% / 47% | the fragment reads come out as four `ds_load_b64`, for which the LDS serves 16 lanes a clock, and a 12-dword pitch is 2-way conflicted; pitch 16 is 4-way |
| `LDS_STAGE` at wave64 4×8, pitch 36 | 256 VGPRs, 54 KB | 36.5 | 70% | conflict-free, still under the shipped build |
| **`LDS_STAGE` at wave32 4×4, pitch 36, one tile a slab** | 8 waves, 240 VGPRs | **40.7** | **78%** | |
| **… two tiles a slab** | 256 VGPRs, 54 KB | **40.9**, o 39.4 | **79%** | **shipped** |
| the wave32 control: 4×4, direct loads, no LDS | 192 VGPRs | 21.5 | 40% | wave32 alone is a disaster: a tile is 272 clocks of MMA and the latency is the same |
| `STAGE_L0`: the staging loads all L0 hits (wrong by construction) | | 44.0 | 82% | **the global side is now ~3 points**; the rest is the LDS-side schedule |

**What it says.**

1. **A wave64 fragment is 8 VGPRs, not 2.** The hardware holds an A or
   B operand four times over (lanes 16–31, 32–47 and 48–63 replicate
   0–15; `coopmat_layout_probe.comp`), so a prefetched *fragment* costs
   4x its bytes in registers and 4x its bytes on the L0-to-VGPR path,
   which is why §6.5's "+24 VGPRs" was +96 and why no register-resident
   prefetch fits beside a 4×8 accumulator grid. At wave32 the replication
   is 2x. A prefetched *slab* costs each byte once, in 24 staging
   registers.
2. **ACO will not carry a fragment across a loop back-edge.** Both
   register-prefetch arms compiled to hundreds of sub-dword moves a
   trip (a coopmat is a vector of 16-bit elements, and a phi it cannot
   coalesce is copied element by element). Any cross-iteration register
   pipelining of cooperative-matrix operands is dead on Mesa 26.2.2, and
   a build that carries none (a hoisted multi-tile slab, or LDS) is the
   only shape that measures the idea. `PIPE` and `DB` stay in
   `dit_gemm.comp` as the record of that, never built.
3. **LDS reads of a cooperative matrix are `ds_load_b64`**, whatever the
   alignment, so the conflict-free pitch is 18 dwords (36 halves) apart
   between the 16 lanes of a fragment, not the 12 (24 halves) that a
   128-bit read would want. §2.1's LDS arms had no pad at all (a pitch
   of 8 dwords, 4-way conflicted) and an [N, K] B gather; that is why
   they lost by 1.2x in 2026-09-14, and it was not the mechanism.
4. **Wave32 and the LDS are one lever, not two.** Wave32 halves the
   bytes every fragment read moves and doubles the accumulator's
   registers, so its largest tile is 4×4 (AI 32) and, fed from global
   memory, its 272-clock tiles expose the same latency four times as
   often: 21.5 TFLOP/s. Fed from LDS a slab ahead, the latency is the
   LDS's and the 4×4 tile's extra bytes go down a path that is not the
   L0's: 40.9.
5. **The gap left is the LDS-side schedule** (~18 points at the clock):
   with the staging loads perfect the kernel reaches 82%; the shipped
   build 79–80%. A slab is 32 MMAs (544 clocks a wave), 64 `ds_load_b64`,
   6 `ds_store`, a barrier and a `lgkmcnt` ladder whose first MMA waits
   for 20 of 64 reads; the issue order of the reads does not move it.
   Where the next 5 points are is open (KERNELS.md G-o8).

**Carry-in (KERNELS.md G8, the same day).** All nine hosts run it,
pinned to wave32 through a `wave` field on each variant table, each
through its own gate: the image DiT's 1024² step 2179 → 2058 ms (one
staging, five arms), the video VAE's 480p decode 37.1 → 35.5 s (its
bias-padded K rounded to 32), Kev's fp16 pass at 494 tokens 154 → 132
ms. **It loses under ~40 workgroups**: ACE's 30 s clip (375 rows, 30
workgroups) is 130 against the wave64 build's 120 ms a forward, ties at
1500 rows, wins 2.5–3% from 3000, so `ace/dit` keeps the wave64 build up
to 1024 rows; parakeet's rung at 138–768 frames loses 8–11%, and its plan
never picks it.

**Reproduce.** The builds are `go generate` lines in `shaders/shaders.go`
(`dit_gemm_wg128x256_lds_w32`, `_krange`); the ladder's arms are
`-DSR`, `-DPIPE`, `-DDB`, `-DLDS_STAGE` with `-DA_LPITCH`/`-DB_LPITCH`,
`-DSTAGE_L0`, `-DLOAD_ORDER` on `dit_gemm.comp`, compiled to the
scratchpad. The screen: `H3_SHAPES=480 H3_PROFILE=1 H3_BANK=q8
H3_GEMM_SPV=path [H3_GEMM_WAVE=32] [H3_GEMM_KRANGE_SPV=path]
H3_GEMM_REF=ref.bits go test ./h3/dit -run TestGPUShapes -v`, where the
first run writes the velocities' bits and every later one is gated on
them; the clock and power from a 100 ms sampler on
`hwmon/freq1_input` and `power1_average` (§0.6). The ISA:
`RADV_DEBUG=asm,shaderstats go run ./cmd/probe build.spv 32`.

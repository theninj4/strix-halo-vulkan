### 2.10 Every VALU instruction costs the matrix pipe a clock: the LDS-staged GEMM's second K tile as a loop — **done** ✅ (2026-09-29)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G-o8, after §2.9): the shipped LDS-staged wave32
GEMM (`dit_gemm_wg128x256_lds_w32`) runs at 80% of the ceiling at its
clock, and with the staging loads all L0 hits at 82%, so the ~18 points
left are on the LDS side of the kernel: 64 `ds_load_b64`, 6 stores, a
barrier and a `lgkmcnt` ladder a slab. Which of those is it?

**Answer: mostly none of them.** The largest single cost was ~40
`v_swap_b32`/`v_mov_b32` a K tile that the register allocator emits to
assemble the slab's *second* tile's fragments, because the scheduler had
hoisted the next slab's six staging loads above this slab's MMAs and the
second tile's `ds_load`s then landed in whatever pairs were free. On this
part **a VALU instruction costs the matrix pipe about one clock** (the
WMMA runs on the SIMD's VALU; there is no separate tensor unit to
overlap with), so those moves were ~7% of the pipe. Keeping the slab's
two K tiles as a loop the compiler may not unroll (`KT_LOOP`), with the
staging views declared writable so NIR does not sink the staging loads to
their use (`STAGE_RW`), takes the moves out: **same MMAs in the same
order, bit-identical**, H3's projections **40.4 → 43.5 TFLOP/s**, the
split down projection 40.3 → 42.2, a 480p forward **27.30 → 26.71 s**,
**80 → 85% per clock**. Shipped as the same build name, so all nine
hosts of the kernel (§2.9's G8 table) get it with no host change.

**The instrument: a probe that adds the kernel's parts back one at a
time.** `shaders/wmma_issue_probe.comp` is the register-only WMMA loop of
`alu_peak.comp` in the GEMM's own geometry (eight wave32 waves of 4×4
tiles, 55 KB of LDS planes at pitch 36), run through `cmd/bench peak` by
the new `PEAK_SPV` hook (`bench/ops_peak.go`), against the same 480
FLOP/clk/CU:

| added to the register-only loop | FLOP/clk/CU | of 480 |
|---|---|---|
| nothing (`wmma_issue_reg`) | 480–491 | 100% |
| 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 independent fp32 FMAs a lane per MMA | 449 / 422 / 382 / 318 | 93.5 / 88 / 80 / 66% |
| a workgroup barrier every 16 MMAs | 480 | 100% |
| the A fragments re-read from LDS each tile (4 a 16 MMAs) | 475 | 99% |
| A and B re-read from LDS (the GEMM's 8 a 16 MMAs, 32 `ds_load_b64`) | 467 | 97% |
| the LDS reads **and** the barrier | 438 | 91% |
| the reads as 16-lane exchanges (`XCHG`: half the LDS bytes, +12 VALU a fragment) | 397 | 83% |
| the exchange and the barrier | 384 | 80% |

Four things it settles. **VALU issue is not free beside the MMAs**: four
FMAs per MMA cost 12%, which is 4 clocks of a 34-clock wave32 MMA, and it
stays linear to 8 a MMA (dual-issue softens 16). So the 16-MMA tile's
budget for everything that is not an MMA is a few instructions, and the
shipped kernel was spending ~50. **The LDS bandwidth is not the limit**:
the GEMM's own read pattern costs 3% (G-o8's "LDS rate for replicated
reads" candidate is dead), and a barrier alone costs nothing. **The two
together cost 9%**: the burst of every wave's reads after the barrier,
which is the lockstep candidate, and which is confirmed on the real kernel
below. And **halving the LDS bytes with `v_permlanex16` loses**: the
exchange is 4 permutes and 8 selects a fragment (ACO lowers
`subgroupShuffleXor(v, 16)` at wave32 to `v_permlanex16_b32`, a VALU op
with no LDS traffic, and the coopmat is assembled from the dwords with no
sub-dword moves), 96 VALU a tile, ~14% of the pipe for a 3% saving.

**How the moves arise, from the disassembly** (`RADV_DEBUG=asm`,
`cmd/probe`, wave32). The shipped slab body issues its six staging
`buffer_load_b128`, then 40 `ds_load_b64` (the first tile's eight
fragments and the second's first two), a `lgkmcnt` ladder with four MMAs
a rung, then the second tile's remaining 24 reads — and in the middle of
it the *next* slab's six staging loads, hoisted across the barrier
(`barrier()` orders shared memory only; a read-only buffer load may move
through it). At 256 VGPRs with 128 of accumulators, 64 of first-tile
fragments and 24 of staging registers there is no contiguous room for the
second tile's 64, so its reads land in the address registers' free pairs
(`v[10:11]`, `v[24:25]`, `v[1:2]`, …) and are `v_swap_b32`'d into
8-register operands behind `s_waitcnt lgkmcnt(N)` steps: 16 swaps and 16
dual moves in one unrolled body, 30 in the other, plus ~17 address ops.
With the tiles as a loop, each iteration is 32 `ds_load_b64`, 2 VALU, 4
waitcnts, 16 MMAs and a branch; the slab's wrapper is the six loads, ~15
address ops and the six `ds_store_2addr_b64`. Two compiler facts on the
way: **NIR sinks a read-only SSBO load to its use** (with the loop in
between, the staging loads went to just before the stores, latency fully
exposed — `nir_opt_access` infers `readonly` back from the absence of a
store, so `STAGE_RW` also plants a never-taken store to each view); and a
buffer-semantics fence on the barrier (`LDS_FENCE`) does not stop the
hoist and changes no instruction.

**What is left, priced.** After this build the gap is ~15 points, and the
arms bound each part: the barrier's lockstep **≤ 9** (`LDS_NOBAR`, an
upper bound: the race also frees the LDS and the non-finite data drops
the power, hence its 2899 MHz), the LDS reads **~4** (`LDS_HALF_READS`
gains 4 with half of them), the global side **~3** (§2.9's `STAGE_L0`).
The exchange cannot buy the 4 (it costs 14 to save 3); a bigger wave tile
cannot (4×8 at wave32 is 256 VGPRs of accumulators); a 128×128 workgroup
that fits three a WGP loses 11% to the global side before its barrier
saving can show; and the LDS geometry admits no third tile a slab
(three-tile planes at any conflict-free pitch are 72–80 KB
double-buffered against a 64 KB workgroup limit). So the remaining lever
is the barrier itself: a slab structure that lets the two workgroups on a
WGP, or the eight waves of one, stay out of phase.

**The rule this adds** (to §2.9's three): on RDNA3.5 **count the VALU
instructions a tile the way the bytes were counted before** — a
`v_swap`, a `v_cndmask`, a `v_permlanex16` and an address add each cost
the matrix pipe a clock, ~3% for every instruction a MMA at wave32. Read
the disassembly for moves before timing a build, and prefer a loop the
compiler cannot reschedule over an unrolled body it will "improve".

The two runs of the shipped build (the second from the embedded SPIR-V,
byte-identical to the screened arm) differ by the clock alone: 407 and
406 FLOP/clk/CU. `TestGPUForward` and every other host's gate (§2.9's G8
table: `h3/vae`, `qimage/dit`, `ace/dit`, `kev`, `parakeet`,
`zimage/qwen`, `ocr`, `ace/lm`) pass on it unchanged.

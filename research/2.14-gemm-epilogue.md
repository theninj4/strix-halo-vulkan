### 2.14 The GEMM's epilogue: the fp32 store costs the pipe nothing, the sawtooth is ≤ 5%, and the two passes that read the fp32 C fused into it — **done** ✅ (2026-09-30)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G3, hypotheses H4 and H5 of the reopened
vertical): the shipped GEMM (`dit_gemm_wg128x256_lds_w32`, §2.9/§2.10)
ends with 16 fp32 accumulator tiles a wave stored row-major — 128
`buffer_store_b32` a lane, two 64 B segments each — and H4 priced that
at up to 9% "if nothing overlaps it". H5 asked what the grid's last
round costs as M varies. And KERNELS.md's table asked for `C_F16` and the
fragment-tile C "where the consumer allows".

**Answers.** (1) **The store costs the matrix pipe under a point.** A
live control that keeps every MMA and skips the stores at run time
(`STORE_TILES`, below) runs the same per-clock rate as the shipped build:
the 9% it reads in TFLOP/s is *all clock*, because a forward whose every
downstream kernel runs on zeros draws 130 W instead of 154 and clocks
2900 instead of 2671. (2) **The sawtooth is ≤ 5%** on the q projection,
≤ 2% on gate and ≤ 3% on down past ~1300 rows, and the served 480p chunks
sit near its top; below ~600 rows the grid's ramp dominates, which is
G8's "the build loses below ~1000 rows". (3) What the fp32 C costs is
**what the consumers do with it afterwards**: on the H3 profile the
SwiGLU pass (2.1% of a forward, reading both fp32 halves) and the v
pack (0.9%, only re-laying out the v projection's C as fragment tiles).
Both are epilogues of the GEMM now, bit-identical to the passes they
replace: **the SwiGLU and pack passes gone, a 480p forward
26.22 / 26.33 → 25.59 / 25.50 s (1.03x)**, the same bits as the shipped
build's forward.

#### H4: the store priced with a live control

`-DSTORE_TILES=n` on `dit_gemm.comp` (a pricing knob, never shipped, wrong
by construction): the first n of a wave's 16 accumulator tiles are stored,
the rest under a condition the compiler cannot decide and that is never
true at run time (`pc.gemmK == 0`), so every MMA stays — the ISA holds 48
`v_wmma` and the same K loop as the shipped build — and one uniform
branch skips the stores. `STORE_TILES=8` leaves half the C stale, which is
*real-looking* data from the chunk before (the arenas hold it), so the
forward's power stays near the cap; `STORE_TILES=0` and `1` leave every
downstream kernel on zeros and the package 24 W under it.

H3 480p (`H3_SHAPES=480 H3_PROFILE=1 H3_BANK=q8`, 15 936 rows in chunks
of 8192; the sampler's median clock and package power over the forward;
per clock = FLOP/clk/CU against 480 at that clock):

| build | qkv | gate / up | o | down | forward | sclk | W | per clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped, run 1 (bit-identical, `H3_GEMM_REF` written) | 43.3 | 43.2 / 43.0 | 42.3 | 42.2 | 26.22 s | 2671 | 154 | 405, **84.4%** |
| shipped, run 2 (bit-identical) | 43.7 | 43.2 / 43.1 | 42.1 | 42.2 | 26.33 | 2672 | 154 | 409, 85.2% |
| `STORE_TILES=8`: half the stores | 44.4 | 44.2 / 44.5 | 43.7 | 44.6 | 25.62 | 2746 | 152 | 404, 84.2% |
| `STORE_TILES=1`: one tile of 16 | 46.3 | 46.3 / 46.3 | 46.2 | 45.2 | 24.40 | 2886 | 143 | 401, 83.6% |
| `STORE_TILES=0`: no store | **47.1** | 47.1 / 47.1 | 46.4 | 45.3 | 24.25 | **2900** | **130** | 406, 84.6% |

The rate climbs 9% as the stores go and the per-clock number does not
move (404–409 at every arm, ±0.6%): the store overlaps the other resident
workgroup's K loop entirely, as G1's "flat from 1 to 3+ workgroups a CU"
predicted it would. H4 is dead on the pipe. What the table also says is
where the store *does* cost: the forward at the cap runs 2671 MHz and the
one that writes nothing 2900, and §0.7's rule is that at the cap a joule
is a clock — so the fp32 C is paid for in the energy of writing it and of
reading it back, not in issue slots, and the way to get it back is fewer
bytes through DRAM, which is the fusion below.

#### H5: the sawtooth

The plateau is reached by ~640 rows on gate (56 column blocks a row
block, 1.4 rounds a step: the last round is never far from full) and by
~1300 on q and down; above it the q rate swings 40.0–44.5 with the
fractional round (a step of 0.7 round: low just past an integer, high
just under one), gate 42.4–44.5, down 40.1–43.0. The served shape's two
chunks (64 and 61 row blocks) land at 43.5–43.9 on q and gate and 41.0–42.6
on down, at the top of each band, which is why the base profile above reads
43.3 / 43.2 / 42.2. Below ~600 rows the ramp and drain of a grid under two
rounds cost 2x, which is what G8 measured as the build losing to the
wave64 one at Kev's 494 rows and `ace/dit`'s short inputs. Nothing to
fix for H3; a host with a free choice of chunk could pick one whose
fractional round lands high on every projection, worth ≤ 2% of the GEMMs.

#### The fusions

Two consumers in H3's block read a GEMM's fp32 C only to narrow it:

- **v → the attention's fragment tiles.** `pack v` (`dit_pack_f16` mode
  1, 244 ms a forward) re-lays the v projection's C out as transposed
  16×16 fp16 tiles a head. `C_PACK` stores the accumulator tile there
  directly (stage 4's "fragment-tile C" item; the code has been in
  `dit_gemm.comp` since PIPELINE.md stage 10, refused on the LDS build by
  a guard that is now lifted), `pc.aux1` the plane's rows, `pc.outOff`
  the chunk's first tile. Two things make it the pack's bits: the fp32 →
  fp16 conversion is the same RTNE narrowing (the pack's scale for v is
  1), and the pad rows — the pack wrote +0 for tokens past the chunk,
  and a −0 behind a zero mask moves a WMMA sum an ulp (K7.7) — are the
  products of zeroed A rows: `zero_f16.comp` zeroes the A operand's rows
  [n, ⌈n/128⌉·128) of the last chunk ahead of the GEMM (the chunk before
  had left its rows there; 50 dispatches, 0 ms), and +0 accumulators plus
  products of +0 stay +0.
- **gate|up → SwiGLU.** `swiglu` (570 ms a forward, 2.1%) read both fp32
  halves (940 MB a chunk, at the DRAM's 247 GB/s) and wrote the fp16 A
  operand of the down projection. Following `kev_gemm_q8_glu.comp` (K7.6):
  the host interleaves the checkpoint's `ff.net.0.proj` as one
  [2·ffn, H] projection (`projGateUp`) in 64-row groups — 32 gate rows,
  then the up rows of the same 32 outputs — so a wave's four accumulator
  tiles are gate 0–1 and up 0–1 of the same columns, and the `C_SWIGLU`
  epilogue is element-wise on two accumulators of one layout:
  `float16_t(g / (1 + exp(-g)) * u * scale)`, the SwiGLU kernel's
  expression in its order, stored row-major at the down projection's A
  stride (`pc.aux0`). The ISA of both lowers it the same way (`v_exp_f32`,
  `v_rcp_f32`, `v_fma_mixlo_f16`), and the K loop of both fused builds is
  the shipped loop instruction for instruction (the diff before the
  epilogue is one `s_mov_b64` for two `s_mov_b32`); 256 VGPRs, no spill.
  The int8 bank stages the interleaved projection like any other, so the
  bank cache's key changes and the served binary restages once.

Both keep a control at run time (`packV`, `fuseGLU` on the GPU; `H3_FUSE=0`
on the screen): the pack, and the SwiGLU pass over the *same fused GEMM's*
fp32 output through its interleaved columns (`dit_swiglu_f16 -DINTERLEAVE=32`),
so `TestGPUForward` compares the two paths bit for bit on the same weights
without restaging (it passes, with `TestGPURun`'s teacher-forced steps; its
negative control for the key-tail zeroing now runs the pack path, since the
fused v's pad keys are +0 by construction). The screen's gate is the shipped build's velocities from
the morning's run (`H3_GEMM_REF`): every fused run is bit-identical to it.

(†) Run 1's forward spent its first 20 s at 1750–1810 MHz and 156–159 W
with nothing in the graph that distinguishes those blocks from the rest,
then ran the remaining 13 s at 2650; neither the control before it nor
the fused run after it shows it, and its per-dispatch profile (taken
after the forward, at 2650) reads the same as run 2's. Recorded as a
transient of the machine (a game process and a `ggml-rpc-server` live on
it), not of the build; the two clean runs stand.

The fused GEMM runs at the same rate as the two it replaces (5.61–5.68 s
against 5.67–5.72), the v store at the same rate as the plain one (1.41–1.42 s
against 1.42), and what the forward gains is the two passes: **0.63–0.83 s
of 26.2–26.3, 1.025–1.03x**, the profile's 0.81 s less the fp16 tiles' and
halves' own stores. Not the energy argument H4's table invited: the fused
forward draws 156 W at 2660–2667 MHz where the shipped draws 154 at 2671, so
the 1.9 GB a chunk of fp32 traffic that is gone did not buy clock —
what it bought is the passes' time.

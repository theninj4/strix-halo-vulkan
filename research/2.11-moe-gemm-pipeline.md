### 2.11 The grouped MoE GEMM's K loop as a software pipeline: the up projection 1.68x at 2048 tokens, bit-identical — **done** ✅ (2026-09-29)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G5): the LLM's prefill spends a quarter of a
token in `llm_moe_gemm.comp`'s up mode, which runs at 22 TFLOP/s executed
(P12: 21.1 useful), ~45% of the matrix pipe at its clock. §2.2 blamed the
LDS round trip the quantised operand takes ("no extension exposes a
fragment's lane layout"); M11c's probe pinned that layout, so G5 was
written as "build the B fragments in registers". Before building it, the
kernel's parts were priced by their absence, the way G1 priced the DiT
GEMM's.

**Answer: the LDS round trip is not the cost; the per-step chain of
dependent, divergent bank loads is, and a pipeline that fetches a step
ahead and reads each block's bytes once takes the up projection from
11.5 to 6.8 ms a layer at 2048 tokens (1.68x), 2.01x at 512 and 1.57x at
4096, bit-identical.** G5's register-fragment idea was not built: with
both stagings removed the loop already runs at the matrix pipe's rate,
so there was nothing for it to buy, and at wave64 it would replicate the
unpack four times over.

**The kernel, from the disassembly** (`cmd/probe`, `RADV_DEBUG=asm`, the
shipped `up_q4k_m4`: one wave64 of a 4×4 tile grid, two 64-column slabs,
BK 32). One K-step is: four permutation loads, four gathered `uvec4` of
the block input into LDS; for each of the lane's two slab columns a
sixteen-byte block header, two `uvec4` of nibbles, ~150 VALU of scale
decode and affine, four `ds_store_b128`; then 96 `ds_load_b64` (24
fragments, 8 VGPRs each) and 64 MMAs. 256 VGPRs, no spill, four
subgroups a SIMD, and **no `s_barrier` at all** (one wave a workgroup;
the compiler drops it). Every load of the step is issued after the
previous step's MMAs and waited for before this step's, and each bank
load is 64 lanes at 64 different rows 1440 bytes apart: sixty-four cache
lines an instruction, of which each lane uses sixteen bytes, and the same
lines again on the next step (a header serves eight steps, a nibble group
two).

**The controls** (`-D` knobs on `llm_moe_gemm.comp`, never shipped, wrong
by construction; `cmd/llm -moe -tokens 2048 -iters 60`, layer 3, the 4k
trace's real routing, the m4 rung; each arm loaded through `LLM_MOE_SPV`;
100 ms clock and package samples, median 2450–2610 MHz at 115–131 W
across the arms, so the ratios stand):

Three things. **The loop without its stagings runs at the pipe**: 255
GFLOP of executed MMAs in 5.33 ms is 47.8 TFLOP/s, against a ceiling of
47–50 at the 2450–2630 MHz the arm sampled — the 24 fragment reads a
step cost ~8% (`MOE_FRAG2` adds them again for 8.5%), and that is the
whole of the "LDS round trip". **The bank side is 30–43% of the step
and most of it is the fetch**: making the loads L0 hits with the same
divergence (`L0_BANK=2`) recovers 1.31x of the 1.44x, so the sixty-four
lines an instruction cost ~4 points and the re-reading of them across
steps and the latency in front of every step cost ~25. **The VALU is a
smaller share than P12 read it as**: the scale path is 1.37x when gutted,
but that arm also deletes the header loads.

**The arm: the K loop as a software pipeline** (`-DPIPE`, the Q4_K up
mode at BK 32). The bytes a lane will unpack at step k+1 — its `uvec4`
of the gathered rows and, per slab column, the block header and the
nibble group — are fetched into registers before step k's MMAs issue and
unpacked from registers at the top of the next trip; the permutation
entries, invariant across K, are read once before the loop. `PIPE=2`
adds the reuse the layout allows: **the header is fetched once per
256-element super-block and the nibble group once per two steps**, so a
super-block costs a lane 9 bank load instructions instead of 24. The
unpack, the LDS slabs and the fragment reads are the shipped ones, so the
result is bit-identical.

| **`PIPE=2`: … and each block's bytes once** | **3 241 (2.01x)** | **6 837 (1.68x)** | **10 925 (1.57x)** |

| `PIPE=1` with two register sets (ping-pong) | 5 035 (m2) | 11 159 (two waves 2×4) / 12 073 (four waves 1×4) | 15 629 |

Executed rate at 2048: 22.1 → 37.3 TFLOP/s (useful, at the m4 rung's
1.90x padding: 11.7 → 19.6). At 512 the m2 rung's `moe.up` is now below
`moe.down`'s 3.0 ms for the first time.

**What the ladder inside the arm says.** `PIPE=1` alone is worth 4–14%:
the loads are in flight behind the MMAs, but the compiler still waits on
them inside the MMA block (`s_waitcnt vmcnt(5)`, `vmcnt(4)` between
`v_wmma`s) because the allocator lands them in temporaries and copies
them to the loop-carried registers there — ~110 `v_mov` a trip, §2.10's
move storm on a different kernel. A two-set form, so the fetch has no
live value to preserve, was built to remove the copies and **spills at
m4** (pre-schedule 359 VGPRs against 256) and buys nothing where it fits
(two-wave 2×4: 11 159; four-wave 1×4: 12 073; m2 at 512: 5 035 against
`PIPE=2`'s 3 241). What made the difference was `PIPE=2`'s reuse: 2.7x
fewer bank load instructions a super-block, none of them re-requesting
lines the L0 just dropped. That is the `L0_BANK=2` control's 1.31x, and
the pipeline then takes the latency on top of it, past the `L0_BANK=1`
arm (8 015) that still issued and waited on three loads a step.

**Register and spill line** (`RADV_DEBUG=shaderstats`, wave64, `PIPE=2`):
m1 216 VGPRs, m2 240, m4 256, w2m1 192, w4m1 96, n1m1 96, n2m1 144; no
spills, no scratch; m4 is 1544 instructions against the shipped 1473.

**Gates.** `TestMoEGPULadderAgrees` (every rung against m2/m2, `maxAbs
== 0`) on each pipelined geometry alone and the whole `TestMoEGPU*` suite
with all seven loaded at once: pass. The shipped `.spv` rebuilt from the
edited source with no knob is byte-for-byte the same instruction stream
(1473 instructions, 256 VGPRs; the SPIR-V differs in id numbering only).

**Refused on the way**: the register-built B fragment (G5 as written —
at wave64 a fragment is 4x replicated, so the unpack would run four
times for a round trip that costs 8%); a wave32 build of the m4 tile
(spills 217); the two-set pipeline (above).

**In the whole model** (`cmd/llm -graph -tokens 2048,4096,8192 -ctx
8192`, 48 layers, `LLM_MOE_BANK`/`LLM_DENSE_BANK` at the shipped plans
with `LLM_BANK_CACHE`, the old and new binaries interleaved twice the same
hour): prefill **1194 / 1206 → 1407 / 1408 tok/s at 2048 tokens (1.17x)**,
1317 / 1339 → 1494 / 1495 at 4096 (1.13x), 1306 / 1345 → 1459 / 1462 at
8192 (1.10x). The share falls with the ubatch because the up projection's
padding factor falls with it (1.90x at 2048, 1.46x at 4096) and the rest
of the token does not move. The embedded builds pass the whole
`TestMoEGPU*` suite; the 1024-token ladder on them (m2 4 487 µs against
m4 4 600) keeps `moeGEMMPlanFor`'s m2/m4 boundary where it was.

**The down mode, measured, does not want it.** The same pipeline written
for the block-32 down formats (B only: its A operand is read straight
from the arena by the fragment loads; Q5_1 six words a step, Q4_1 five,
Q8_0 ten, IQ4_NL its nine-word pair once per two steps under `PIPE=2`),
every build spill-free and the whole `TestMoEGPU*` suite passing with all
twenty loaded:

| `moe.down`, µs a layer, m4 (m2 at 512) | 512 | 2048 | 4096 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Q5_1 (the checkpoint's; layer 3) shipped | 2 995 / 2 982 | 6 587 / 6 582 | 9 507 |
| Q5_1 `PIPE=2` | 3 000 | 6 607 / 6 613 | 9 487 |
| IQ4_NL (the served format, `LLM_MOE_BANK=down_exps=iq4_nl`) shipped | 2 718 | 5 634 | 8 264 |
| **IQ4_NL `PIPE=2`** | **2 266 (1.20x)** | **5 366 (1.05x)** | **7 946 (1.04x)** |

Q5_1 moves by nothing, though its `L0_BANK` control read 1.29x — so what
that control removed was not latency the pipeline could hide but the
load's own cost, and a 32-element block a step has no reuse to hoist.
IQ4_NL gains exactly what its pair layout allows, the second half of the
pair read from a register instead of re-fetched. The IQ4_NL builds ship
with `PIPE=2` (whole-model prefill 1408 → 1422 tok/s at 2048 and 1502 →
1507 at 4096 on top of the up projection's gain, two runs each); Q5_1,
Q4_1 and Q8_0 keep the plain loop. The down mode's
remaining cost sits with its A fragment loads (sixteen `buffer_load_b128`
a step, issued inside the MMA block and not pipelined: a fragment cannot
be carried across the loop without §2.9's element-by-element copy).

**What is left.** The Q5_K up build (layer 2 of 48) keeps the plain loop.
The plan chooser (`moeGEMMPlanFor`) was re-measured on the pipelined
builds and its m2/m4 boundary stands (m2 4 487 µs against m4 4 600 at
1024 tokens). The executed rate at 2048 is now 37 TFLOP/s of a ~49
ceiling with a 1.90x padding factor on top: the next lever on the up
kernel is the padding, not the loop, and on the down kernel the A
fragment loads.

**Reproduce.** The shipped builds are the seven `llm_moe_up_q4k_*` and
the five `llm_moe_down_iq4nl_*` generate lines in `shaders/shaders.go`
with `-DPIPE=2`. The controls are
`-DMOE_NO_UNPACK=1`, `-DMOE_NO_ASTAGE=1`, `-DMOE_NIB_ONLY=1`,
`-DMOE_L0_BANK=1|2`, `-DMOE_L0_A=1`, `-DMOE_FRAG2=1`, `-DWAVE=32` on
`llm_moe_gemm.comp`, compiled to the scratchpad and loaded with
`LLM_MOE_SPV=up_q4k_m4=path.spv` (comma-separated for several,
`LLM_MOE_SPV_WAVE=32` for a wave32 build) into `cmd/llm -moe` or the
`llm` tests, which take the same override. The clock beside it: a 100 ms
loop over `hwmon/freq1_input` and `power1_average`; a `-moe` arm is
about two seconds of device time, so quote the median of the samples
above 100 W.

### 2.12 The grouped MoE GEMM's short last tile: the alignment one fragment, the loop one of WM copies — **done** ✅ (2026-09-30)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G5, after §2.11): the up projection's executed
rate at 2048 tokens is 37 TFLOP/s of a ~49 ceiling, and on top of it sits
a **1.90x padding factor**: the permutation rounds every expert's row
range up to the widest row block in the plan (64), so 608 tiles execute
38 912 rows for 20 480 real ones. P11-4 tried three ways of padding to the
fragment instead and every one was a wash or a loss, because *then* the
kernel was bound by its slab unpack and the rows were nearly free. §2.11's
pipeline made the MMAs ~78% of the kernel. Are the rows the cost now?

**Answer: yes, and the down projection's more than the up's.** The
alignment is now sixteen rows, the permutation's record already said how
many of a block's rows are real, and the single-wave builds of
`llm_moe_gemm.comp` are `SHORT` builds that run one of WM copies of their
K loop and store chosen by a uniform switch on that count — no bound inside
the unrolled loops, the same MMAs per real row in the same order, so it is
**bit-identical** to the padded schedule (`TestMoEGPULadderAgrees` at
`maxAbs == 0` across every mixed plan, `TestGraphLogits`'s whole-model
diagnostics reproduced to the digit against the old build). At 2048
tokens `moe.up` **6 994 / 7 029 → 6 314 / 6 255 µs a layer (1.11x)**,
`moe.down` **6 577 / 6 609 → 5 370 / 5 339 (1.23x)** on the checkpoint's
Q5_1 and **5 373 → 4 209 (1.28x)** on the served IQ4_NL, the block
**1.13–1.14x**; whole-model prefill **1 417 / 1 419 → 1 514 / 1 513 tok/s
at 2048 (1.07x)**, 1 500 / 1 502 → 1 549 / 1 543 at 4096, 1 461 / 1 467 →
1 481 / 1 483 at 8192. And with the padding no longer a price on the wide
rung, the plan's m2/m4 boundary moves from 1024 tokens to 256.

**What the padding was.** `Schedule()` on the 4k trace's real routing,
executed rows over real (the tile count is the same at both alignments,
because an expert's last block is still a block):

| tokens | rows | pad 64 (was) | pad 16 | pad 32 (what the up m4 build executes) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 5 120 | 11 904 (2.33x) | 8 064 (1.57x) | 11 904 at the m2 rung's own 32 |
| 1024 | 10 240 | 17 536 (1.71x, m2) | 13 472 (1.32x) | 17 536 |
| 2048 | 20 480 | 38 912 (1.90x) | 24 304 (1.19x) | 28 832 (1.41x) |
| 4096 | 40 960 | 59 904 (1.46x) | 44 816 (1.09x) | 49 536 (1.21x) |

**The arm.** The record's third word has carried the block's real rows
since C5 (the decode GEMV skips its padding with it); the GEMM never read
it. A SHORT build rounds it up to the schedule's alignment (`pc.gemmM`,
`MoEGPU.pad`, so a plan that mixes in a multi-wave rung and aligns to 64
still runs whole blocks) and takes `wmEff = ceil(rows / 16)`; the K loop
and the epilogue are macros over the tile count and the kernel holds one
instantiation per height behind `switch (wmEff)`. The pipeline's first
fetch, the permutation entries and the accumulators' zeroing sit before
the switch and are shared. MODE 0's A staging still fills all 64 rows —
the loads are in flight behind the MMAs, and the rows past the block are
valid entries of the permuted space (the permutation sentinel-fills to a
whole 64-row block past its end, which `maxRows` has room for) — so only
the MMAs and the stores of the missing tiles go. `llm_moe_perm.comp` cuts
`ceil(padded / bm)` records an expert instead of `padded / bm`; the
host's `pad()` is the max over the plan's rungs of `moeAlign` (16 for a
single-wave rung or a GEMV, the block for `w2m1`/`w4m1`), `maxTiles` has a
pad-below-block form, the tile arenas are sized for the widest alignment
whatever the plan, and the shared expert's host-written list gets a short
last block the same way. `LLM_MOE_PAD=16|32|64` forces the alignment for a
screen.

Any third copy of the Q4_K up loop spills eleven to fifteen registers
that its epilogue needs across the K loop (the scratch stores sit before
the first copy's loop, the reloads among its sigmoid), and a control run
of the four-copy build with the alignment forced to 64 — every block
whole, every workgroup in the 4-tile copy — is **9% slower than the
shipped kernel on the same work** (7 393 against 6 798 µs), which is the
spill and the code. The two-copy form costs nothing on full blocks and
rounds a 16-row tail up to 32 rows of MMA; **its store is still bounded by
the real count**, because the rows past the block are the next expert's,
and the first cut that stored the rounded tiles was a race the ladder test
caught at once. So the up m4 build executes rows as at an alignment of
32, and the down builds, with their 120–144 registers, keep all four
copies and execute exactly the alignment-16 rows.

**The screen** (`LLM_MOE_SPV` on `cmd/llm -moe -tokens 512,1024,2048,4096
-iters 60`, layer 3, the real routing, Q5_1 down; 100 ms clock and package
samples, median 2 700–2 850 MHz at 146–149 W across the arms):

| 2048 tokens, µs a layer | rows | `moe.up` | `moe.down` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped (§2.11) | 1.90x | 6 798 | 6 589 |
| SHORT four copies, `LLM_MOE_PAD=64` (the control) | 1.90x | 7 393 | 6 553 |
| SHORT four copies, alignment 16 | 1.19x | 6 138 | 5 345 |
| SHORT two copies, alignment 32 | 1.41x | 6 183 | 5 691 |

**Why the up projection keeps less than the rows it sheds.** On the same
four-copy build, taking the alignment from 64 to 16 removes 14 608 rows for
1 255 µs — **0.086 µs a row**, against the 0.137 µs a row §2.11's MMA-only
control put on a full tile. A short tile multiplies its rows against the
same slab unpack as a full one: two 64-column slabs a K-step, ~300 VALU
instructions a lane, each of which costs the matrix pipe a clock (§2.10),
so ~600 clocks beside the 544 clocks of a 16-row tile's sixteen MMAs where
a full tile's 64 MMAs are 2 176. The rows a short tile drops are cheaper
rows. It is also why the m4 rung's two-copy form loses so little to the
four-copy one (6 183 against 6 138): the 16-row tail it rounds to 32 was
never going to run at the pipe. The down mode unpacks one slab a step, so
its rows are dearer and its gain larger.

**The plan.** The rung ladder on the SHORT builds (`-ladder -iters 30`,
block µs, `m2/m2` against `m4/m4`): 3 075 / 3 126 at 64 tokens, 3 822 /
3 869 at 128, 4 737 / 4 735 at 256, **6 460 / 6 380 at 512, 9 361 / 8 999
at 1024** — the wide rung no longer pays for its width in rows, only in
the tail its two copies round up, so `moeGEMMPlanFor` switches to m4 above
256 tokens instead of above 1024. The mixed pairs are within 1% of the
diagonal at every count; the multi-wave rungs, still aligned to their
block, lose 1.2–1.6x at every count and stay out of the plan. On the plan
as shipped (`results/g5c_moe_short_r{1,2}.csv`, 60 iters, two runs) the
block is **6 312 / 6 313 µs at 512 tokens** against the old schedule's
6 805 / 6 833 (1.08x) and **8 800 / 8 810 at 1024** against 9 643 / 9 671
(1.10x), 13 876 / 13 767 at 2048, 22 615 / 22 703 at 4096; with the
served IQ4_NL down 5 508, 7 879 / 7 870, 12 628 / 12 633 and 21 420 /
21 371.

**In the whole model** (`cmd/llm -graph -tokens 2048,4096,8192 -ctx 8192`,
48 layers, the shipped banks under `LLM_BANK_CACHE`, old and new binaries
interleaved twice): **1 416.5 / 1 418.6 → 1 514.0 / 1 513.2 tok/s at 2048
(1.07x)**, 1 499.7 / 1 502.1 → 1 549.4 / 1 542.8 at 4096 (1.03x), 1 461.4 /
1 466.7 → 1 480.8 / 1 482.7 at 8192 (1.01x); the share falls with the
ubatch as the padding factor did. `TestGraphLogits` on the new test binary
prints the same `result_output` line to the digit as on the old one
(`maxAbs 1.987e+00 at 19208, rms 3.314e-01`, argmax 11751 against 561,
0.4920 apart).

**What is left on this kernel.** The short tiles are bound by the slab
unpack, which is now the largest term in the up projection at every rung:
~300 VALU a lane a K-step for two slabs, every instruction a matrix clock.
That is G5's remaining argument for `v_perm`/`v_cvt_pk` sequences over
per-element work (P12-3 priced the scale path at 1.37x when gutted, header
loads included), and it would lift the short tiles most. The down mode's A
fragment loads inside its MMA block (§2.11) are the other.

**Reproduce.** The 27 single-wave `llm_moe_*` generate lines in
`shaders/shaders.go` carry `-DSHORT=1`, the Q4_K up m4 line
`-DSHORT_STEP=2`. `LLM_MOE_PAD=64` with the SHORT builds is the
whole-block control; a pre-§2.12 build loaded through `LLM_MOE_SPV` needs
it or it writes the next expert's rows. The runs above are
`results/g5c_moe_short_r{1,2}.csv` (Q5_1 down) and
`results/g5c_moe_short_iq4nl_r{1,2}.csv` (the served format), on the plan
as shipped.

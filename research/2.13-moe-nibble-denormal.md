### 2.13 The Q4_K nibble as an f16 denormal: the slab unpack's VALU 331 → 259 a step, bit-identical, `moe.up` 1.02x — and the move storm and the A prefetch priced as dead — **done** ✅ (2026-09-30)

**Question** (KERNELS.md G5, after §2.12): the up projection's short tiles
are bound by the slab unpack, "~300 VALU a lane a K-step, every
instruction a matrix clock" (§2.10). §2.11 left G5's argument for
`v_perm`/`v_cvt_pk` sequences over per-element work. What does the unpack
actually spend per element, and how much of it can go without moving a
bit of the slab?

**Answer: less than the estimate hoped.** Read from the ISA, an element of
the shipped unpack is three VALU instructions, not five: the compiler
already fuses the fma and the half conversion into one `v_fma_mixlo_f16`
/ `v_fma_mixhi_f16`, so what stands in front of it is a `v_bfe_u32` and a
`v_cvt_f32_u32`. Both go: **a halfword whose low nibble is the value and
whose other twelve bits are zero *is* the half `nib × 2^-24`**, an exact
denormal, and `v_fma_mix` reads a half from either half of a word for
free (op_sel). A word's four nibbles become two masked words — `w &
0x000F000F` holds elements 0 and 2, `(w >> 8) & mask` elements 1 and 3 —
three instructions for four elements instead of eight, and the scale
carries the 2^24 (2^20 for the high nibble, whose halfword is `nib ×
16`). `d × sc` is an 11-by-6-bit product, exact in fp32, and a power of
two keeps it exact, so the fma sees the same operands to the bit and the
slab is byte-identical. The full four-tile K-step's non-MMA VALU goes
**331 → 259** (of ~2 176 clocks of MMA), and `moe.up` at 2048 tokens
**6 236 / 6 298 → 6 180 / 6 115 µs (1.02x)**, 1.013x at 512, 1.02x at
1024, 1.024x at 4096. Shipped on the seven Q4_K up builds (`-DNIB_F16=1`).
Two other arms on the same step measured dead: keeping the two K tiles as
a loop (§2.10's fix) *adds* moves here (38 a tile from the allocator), and
fetching A at the top of the step instead of a step ahead removes 65 of
the 71 moves and costs **12%**, because the exposed A latency is worth
far more than the moves.

Whole shader: 2 504 → 2 401 instructions, 256 VGPRs, no spill, at every
build. The same knob on the other rungs: m1 216 VGPRs, m2 240, n2m1 144,
n1m1 96 — and **those loops carry 4–6 moves a step where the m4 carries
71**. The move storm is register pressure at 256, not the unroll.

**Why 2% and not the 6% the count predicts for a short tile.** 72 VALU
of ~2 500 clocks is 2.9% at a full tile and 5% at the two-tile copy the
up build executes its tails as; the measured 2% says the unpack's VALU
was only partly on the critical path — the four waves a SIMD overlap one
wave's unpack with another's MMAs some of the time, which §2.10's probe
(one kernel, VALU *between* its own MMAs) does not see. The count is the
upper bound on what a VALU cut buys here, not its price.

**What the two dead arms say about the rest of the step.** The 71 moves
are the allocator assembling eight-register fragments out of scattered
`ds_load_b64` pairs at a 256-VGPR limit; the sixteen registers of the A
prefetch are the difference between that and the m2 build's four moves,
and taking them (fetch A from the hoisted permutation entries at the top
of each step, `PIPE_A=0`) removes the moves and loses 12%: the A latency
the pipeline hides is worth ~6x the moves it costs. So the moves stay,
bounded at ~3% (71 clocks of ~2 500), and the up projection's step is
now: 64 MMAs, 64 fma_mix (the floor), ~50 mask instructions, ~70 moves
that cannot be bought, ~75 of scale path and addresses, 24 fragment
reads (~8%, §2.11) and 12 LDS stores. There is no 5% left in the unpack.

**Gates.** `TestMoEGPULadderAgrees` with the new m4 loaded through
`LLM_MOE_SPV` against the embedded (old) m2/m2: `maxAbs == 0` on every
mixed plan (so RADV preserves fp16 denormals and `v_fma_mix`'s half
input conversion honours them on this part — a build that flushed would
have failed with every value `-dm`); `TestMoEGPUPaddingIsInert` passes;
the negative control (a `MOE_NIB_ONLY` build through the same override)
fails the same gate, so the override was live. With the seven builds
regenerated: the whole `TestMoEGPU*` suite passes, and `TestGraphLogits`
prints the same `result_output` line to the digit on the old and new test binaries (`maxAbs 1.987e+00 at 19208, rms 3.314e-01`, argmax 11751 against 561, 0.4920 apart).

**Refused on the way.** Packed-f16 math for the affine (`v_pk_fma_f16`,
two elements an instruction) is not bit-identical — `d × sc` rounds to
eleven bits — and the f16 magic-number forms (`0x6400 | nib` = 1024 +
nib) put a `1024 × ds` term into the constant whose rounding is larger
than the result's; `v_cvt_f32_ubyte0..3` on a masked word is one
instruction an element where the denormal is three for four; a
16-entry per-block table is a register-indexed lookup, which is a
`v_cndmask` chain. Q5_K (layer 2 of 48) and Q8_0 (the shared expert,
signed bytes: a denormal has no sign to give) keep the shipped unpack.

**Reproduce.** `-DNIB_F16=1` on the seven `llm_moe_up_q4k_*` generate
lines in `shaders/shaders.go`; `-DPIPE_A=0` and `-DMOE_KT_LOOP=1` are the
dead arms, kept as knobs. The screen is `cmd/llm -moe -model
models/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-UD-Q4_K_XL-00001-of-00004.gguf
-tokens 512,1024,2048,4096 -iters 60` with each arm through
`LLM_MOE_SPV=up_q4k_m4=…` (the model must be the shard: the directory
holds four checkpoints and the bench refuses to pick).
